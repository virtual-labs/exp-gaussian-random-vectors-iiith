Instructions

Theory: The 2D Gaussian distribution extends the concept to two random variables. It is defined by a mean vector [μx, μy] and a 2x2 covariance matrix. This matrix must be symmetric and positive-definite.

Procedure:

  1. Set the center of the distribution using the "Mean Vector" inputs.
  2. Define the shape and orientation of the distribution with the "Covariance Matrix" inputs. Note that σxy must equal σyx.
  3. The variances (σx², σy²) control the spread along the axes.
  4. The covariance (σxy) controls the correlation between the variables and the tilt of the distribution.
  5. Press the "Update 2D Gaussian" button to visualize the changes. The plots will automatically re-center on the mean.

Goals:

  • Understand how the mean vector moves the entire distribution in the 2D plane.
  • Observe how changing σx² and σy² stretches the distribution along the x and y axes.
  • See how a non-zero covariance (σxy) creates correlation and tilts the elliptical contours. A positive covariance tilts up, a negative one tilts down.
