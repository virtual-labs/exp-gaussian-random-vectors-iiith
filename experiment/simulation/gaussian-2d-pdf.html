2D Gaussian PDF

Instructions

Theory: The 2D Gaussian distribution extends the concept to two random variables. It is defined by a mean vector \([\mu_x, \mu_y]\) and a 2x2 covariance matrix \(\Sigma\). This matrix must be symmetric and positive-definite.

Procedure:

  1. Set the center of the distribution using the "Mean Vector" inputs.
  2. Define the shape and orientation of the distribution with the "Covariance Matrix" inputs. Note that \(\sigma_{xy}\) must equal \(\sigma_{yx}\).
  3. The variances (\(\sigma_x^2, \sigma_y^2\)) control the spread along the axes.
  4. The covariance (\(\sigma_{xy}\)) controls the correlation between the variables and the tilt of the distribution.
  5. Press the "Update 2D Gaussian" button to visualize the changes. The plots will automatically re-center on the mean.

Goals:

  • Understand how the mean vector moves the entire distribution in the 2D plane.
  • Observe how changing \(\sigma_x^2\) and \(\sigma_y^2\) stretches the distribution along the x and y axes.
  • See how a non-zero covariance (\(\sigma_{xy}\)) creates correlation and tilts the elliptical contours. A positive covariance tilts up, a negative one tilts down.
