Generate Realizations

Instructions

Theory: A key property of the Gaussian distribution is the percentage of data that falls within a certain number of standard deviations (σ) from the mean. For a standard normal distribution (μ=0, σ=1), approximately 95.45% of all values lie within the interval [-2σ, +2σ]. This is part of the 68-95-99.7 rule.

Procedure:

  1. This experiment simulates drawing random samples from a standard Normal Distribution.
  2. Use the slider to select the number of samples to generate and click the button.
  3. The generated samples are plotted as vertical lines. Lines in green fall within the [-2σ, +2σ] range; lines in red fall outside.
  4. The statistics below the slider show the actual percentage of samples that fell within the range for your current run.

Goals:

  • Observe that for a small number of samples, the measured percentage can be far from the theoretical 95.45%.
  • See how, as you increase the number of samples, the measured percentage consistently gets closer to 95.45%. This is a demonstration of the Law of Large Numbers.
