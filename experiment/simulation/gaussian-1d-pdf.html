Instructions

Theory: The one-dimensional Gaussian (or Normal) distribution is a fundamental continuous probability distribution. It is defined by two parameters: the mean (μ), which represents the central location, and the variance (σ²), which measures the spread or dispersion of the data. The probability density function (PDF) is given by the formula:

\(f(x | \mu, \sigma^2) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{ - \frac{ (x - \mu)^2 }{ 2\sigma^2 } }\)

Procedure:

  1. Use the "Mean (μ)" slider to shift the distribution left or right.
  2. Use the "Variance (σ²)" slider to change the spread of the distribution.
  3. Based on the current variance, calculate the peak height of the PDF using the formula (when x=μ).
  4. Enter your calculated value in the "Calculate the Height" box and click "Check Answer" to verify.

Goals:

  • Observe how the mean (μ) corresponds to the x-coordinate of the peak.
  • Understand that a smaller variance (σ²) leads to a taller, narrower peak, indicating higher certainty.
  • Understand that a larger variance (σ²) leads to a shorter, wider peak, indicating greater uncertainty.
